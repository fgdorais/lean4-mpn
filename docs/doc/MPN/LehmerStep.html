    Lehmer's step on arrays of limbs #

    The cofactors of a run of Euclid's steps on the leading words of two numbers a ≥ b give a matrix of words S = [[|D|, |B|], [|C|, |A|]] and the numbers x = |A a + B b| and y = |C a + D b|, each in one pass (LinComb.mulSubMul), with (a, b) = S (x, y) (applyW). Only words are checked: the instance turns the cofactors into a matrix whose entries are below 2ᶜ and whose determinant is ±1 (wmat), and both differences must be nonnegative, which makes the relation exact; nothing about the cofactors needs a proof. The matrices are a structure of the instance's (W), whose fields are unboxed words: a structure over the limbs would box each entry, an allocation per entry and per step on 64-bit limbs, which made gcd 10% slower.

    stepW is such a step from the instance's cofactors, lehmerStep its two numbers without the matrix, which have the same greatest common divisor as a and b, and mulMatW the product of a matrix of numbers by a matrix of words (LinComb.mulAddMul). smallSteps makes of them the base case of half-gcd (NatOps.SmallSteps) for any representation that converts to and from the arrays.
