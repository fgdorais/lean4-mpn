    Divide-and-conquer division, at any base #

    NatOps.divNorm base T U V n k divides U < V Bᵏ by a divisor V < Bⁿ by computing the quotient in digits of at most ⌊n / 2⌋ limbs from the top down, like Algorithm D in base Bˢ: the scheme of Burnikel and Ziegler and of GMP's mpn_dcpi1_div_qr, written recursively. The top digit of s limbs is D = ⌊W / V⌋ < Bˢ, where W = ⌊U / Bʲ⌋ and j = k - s. It is estimated from the top 2 s limbs Wₕ of W and the top s limbs Vₕ of V as ⌊Wₕ / Vₕ⌋, a recursive division, or Bˢ - 1 when that would not be a digit; the estimate is never too small. Like GMP, the step keeps the remainder R₀ of the recursive division: with W = Wₕ P + W_lo and V = Vₕ P + V_lo for P = B^(n-s), W - Q₀ V = (R₀ P + W_lo) - Q₀ V_lo, so it multiplies Q₀ by the low n - s limbs of V only, and dcFix adds V back while the difference is negative. The rest, U - D V Bʲ < V Bʲ, is divided recursively, and below T limbs in V the instance's base division takes over. Division then costs O(M(n) log n).

    The result is exact for any V > 0. When V is normalized, with its top limb at least B / 2, the estimate is at most two too large and dcFix takes at most two steps; the instance normalizes before calling, with its own shifts. From main64's Num.divNorm.
