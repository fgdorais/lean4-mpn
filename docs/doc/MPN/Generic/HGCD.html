    Half-gcd, at any base #

    The subquadratic reduction of a pair of numbers for the greatest common divisor, after Schönhage and Möller (GMP's mpn_hgcd): the matrix of Euclid's steps that halves a ≥ b is computed from their top halves, recursively, and applied to the full numbers with products. A matrix of determinant ±1 keeps the greatest common divisor whatever its entries (gcd_applyInv), so none of the matrices needs a proof of how well it reduces: the caller only checks that the numbers got smaller. Matrices have nonnegative entries and the sign of their determinant, and their determinant stays ±1 by construction, as products of elementary matrices and of the matrices of a base case, which the instance provides with a proof of its determinant and exact relation.

                                    Type 1

                                    The operations of a base case of half-gcd by matrices of small entries, words for example, which the instance applies to its numbers in its own way: step a b is such a matrix S and the numbers (x, y) with (a, b) = S (x, y), from Lehmer's steps on the leading words of a and b, and mulMat M S is the product M S.                                           Half-gcd: the matrix of Euclid's steps that brings a ≥ b, of n limbs, below Bˢ with s = ⌊n / 2⌋ + 1, or close to it, and the numbers it brings them to. Up to T limbs by the instance's base s a b (stepBase, for one); above, the matrix and numbers of the top n - s limbs, recursively, extended to a and b with products by their low s limbs (applySplit), then one step of Euclid's algorithm, then the same for the top limbs of what is left. A matrix from top limbs that went too far for the full numbers is dropped. Only the determinant and the exact relation are proved (hgcd_ok): the caller checks how far the numbers came down.
