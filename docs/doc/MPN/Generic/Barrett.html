    Division by a reciprocal, at any base #

    Barrett's division of x by an n-limb divisor d with μ ≈ B²ⁿ / d: the quotient estimate q = ⌊⌊x / Bⁿ⁻¹⌋ μ / Bⁿ⁺¹⌋ costs one product, the remainder x - q d another, and a good μ leaves q at most a few units below the quotient. Longer dividends are divided n limbs at a time (divBlocks). The reciprocal comes from Newton's iteration on the top half of d (recip), at about the cost of a product.

    None of the estimates needs a proof: fixQuot checks every quotient estimate exactly against x = q d + r with 0 ≤ r < d, corrects it a few units up or down, and falls back to the slow division of NatOps when it is further off. The specifications therefore hold for any μ; the quality of μ only decides how fast they are met.

    divMuW saves part of the remainder's product. When d μ ≤ B²ⁿ < d (μ + 5) for a normalized d, the remainder x - q d is less than 7 d, so it is its residue modulo B^N - 1 for N > n, which mulMod, a wrapped product, computes faster (barrettW). That bound on μ is checked rather than assumed: recipG makes every Newton step with a wrapped product d μₕ, whose error term e = B^(n+h) - d μₕ is less than Bⁿ⁺¹ when μₕ is good, hence exact modulo B^N - 1; the step then checks its own result (recipG_spec). When a check fails, divMuW is divMu.

    divQMuW computes the quotient alone. Barrett's estimate of the quotient of x B, one limb more, decides ⌊x / d⌋ without the last block's remainder unless its last limb is within 7 of B (divQBlocksW); then it divides as divMuW.

            def MPN.NatOps.recip {α : Type} (o : NatOps α) (d : α) :
            α

            An approximation of B²ⁿ / d for the n-limb d, by Newton's iteration: the reciprocal μₕ of the top h = ⌈n / 2⌉ + 1 limbs, then one step from μ₀ = μₕ Bᵏ, k = n - h: μ₀ + μ₀ (B²ⁿ - d μ₀) / B²ⁿ = μₕ Bᵏ + μₕ e / B²ⁿ⁻²ᵏ with e = B²ⁿ⁻ᵏ - d μₕ, an n by h product. e is about B²ⁿ⁻ᵏ⁻ʰ, and only its limbs from t = 2n - 2k - h - 2 on enter the correction, an h by k product. The step squares the relative error of μ₀, about B⁻ʰ, so the error of the result is about Bⁿ⁻²ʰ ≤ B⁻² units: the extra limb of h keeps errors from growing from one level to the next. Four limbs and fewer by the slow division.

                Barrett's division with wrapped remainders #

                When the reciprocal is good, d μ ≤ B²ⁿ < d (μ + 5) for a normalized d, Barrett's estimate is at most 6 below the quotient, so that the remainder x - q d is less than 7 d: it is then its residue modulo B^N - 1 for N > n, and mulMod computes q d modulo B^N - 1 faster than the product. Whether the reciprocal is good is checked rather than proved: each Newton step of recipG, whose error term e = B^(n+h) - d μₕ shows how good its result is, gives d μ ≤ B²ⁿ < d (μ + 5) when |e| < Bⁿ⁺¹, and it checks that bound. Otherwise divMuW falls back on divMu.

                      The quotient alone #

                      Barrett's estimate of the quotient of a B is at most 6 below it, so its last limb decides the quotient of a unless it is within 6 of B: then the estimate and the quotient of a B have the same limbs above the last. The quotient of a comes without the remainder of the last block, and its product, except in that rare case.

                            A reciprocal of the top limbs #

                            GMP's mpn_mu_div_qr and mpn_mu_divappr_q divide by a reciprocal shorter than the divisor: for a quotient of n limbs by n limbs, one of about n / 2 limbs, of the top limbs of the divisor, with the quotient in two blocks of that size. Newton's iteration on half the limbs costs half as much, and the products of the blocks are half as long.

                            D = ⌊d / Bⁿ⁻ᵏ⁻¹⌋ + 1 (topD) is the top k + 1 limbs of the n-limb d, plus one, so that d < D Bⁿ⁻ᵏ⁻¹, and μ its reciprocal from recipG. For x < 2 Bᵏ d, the estimate ⌊⌊x / Bⁿ⁻¹⌋ μ / Bᵏ⁺²⌋ is at most 7 below ⌊x / d⌋ (trunc_bound): the remainder is less than 8 d, its residue modulo B^N - 1 for N > n (barrettT).
