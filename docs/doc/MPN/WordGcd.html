    The greatest common divisor on words #

    Euclid's algorithm on UInt64 (wordGcd), and the runs of Euclid's steps on the leading words of two numbers that Lehmer's algorithm takes (lehmerSteps), with their cofactors. Both libraries apply the cofactors to their numbers in their own way and check them there, so nothing is proved about lehmerSteps.

        def MPN.lehmerSteps (x y A B C D : Int64) :
        Nat → Cofactors

        Euclid's algorithm on the leading words x ≥ y of two numbers, with the cofactors A, B, C, D of the steps so far, like GMP's hgcd2 on one word: it stops when the remainder falls below 2³¹, half the bits of the words, which keeps the cofactors below 2³¹, or when Jebelean's condition fails, which makes the quotients those of the full numbers. A quotient of 1, the most common, costs a comparison rather than a division. It computes in Int64, since Int boxes values from 2³¹. Nothing is proved about it: the caller checks the cofactors it returns, and takes a step of Euclid's algorithm when they do not apply.
