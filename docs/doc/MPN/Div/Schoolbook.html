    Schoolbook division, for limbs of any width #

    The core of Knuth's Algorithm D, like GMP's schoolbook division, on arrays of limbs of w bits, β = 2ʷ, over WordArray and WordFolds: one quotient limb per step from the top down. At the step for window i, the remainder R so far is below v βⁱ⁺¹, so the quotient limb is ⌊W / v⌋ < β, where W is the window of limbs i to i + n of R.

    The limb is estimated by qEst from the top three limbs of W and the top two of v, as in Algorithm D. The step subtracts the estimate times v from the window in place, and adds v back while the window is negative (addBack). Correctness only needs the estimate never to be too small, which qEst_ge proves; that it is rarely too large, when the top limb of v is at least β / 2, is only a matter of speed, and the callers normalize the divisor for it. Given the reciprocal of the top two limbs of v, the estimate is instead GMP's 3-by-2 step (qEstR), checked exactly, with qEst when a check fails, so that neither the reciprocal nor the error of the step needs a proof.

    The division works in one array (divLoopIn): after the step for window i, its remainder is below v, so the top limb i + n of the window is free, and the step writes the quotient limb there (divStepIn). The multiply-subtract pass is the in-place fold foldThen, ended by a continuation, so that a step allocates nothing unless the estimate was too large. At the end the limbs below n are the remainder and the limbs from n on the quotient. The divisor is another array, read from an offset.

    For a divisor of at least t₂ limbs (the widths' div2Rows), the division takes two quotient limbs per step (divLoop2In), with passes like GMP's mpn_submul_2: the two limbs are estimated together from the top five limbs of a window of n + 2 limbs and the top three of v (qEst2R: two checked 3-by-2 steps and Knuth's test by the third limb), and one pass subtracts v times both, with a borrow of two limbs (subMul2B), so that each limb of the window is read and written once for two products. A window that goes negative is corrected by addBack2, and when a check fails the step falls back to two steps of one limb.

        The estimate with a precomputed reciprocal #

        GMP's schoolbook division estimates each quotient limb without the hardware divider, by Möller and Granlund's 3-by-2 step (udiv_qr_3by2) with the reciprocal ⌊(β³ - 1) / (v₁ β + v₂)⌋ - β of the top two limbs of the normalized divisor (invert_pi1). qEstR checks the quotient and remainder of the step exactly, q (v₁ β + v₂) + r = w₂ β² + w₁ β + w₀ with r < v₁ β + v₂, by four products off the dependency chain; then q is the quotient of the top three limbs by the top two, which is never too small. Otherwise it takes qEst. So neither the reciprocal nor the error bound of the step needs a proof, and the callers compute the reciprocal their own way.

                  The estimate of two quotient limbs #

                  The quotient of the top four limbs of a window of n + 2 limbs by the top two of the divisor is never smaller than the two quotient limbs of the window, and for a normalized divisor it is rarely larger. qEst2 computes it by two 3-by-2 steps with the reciprocal, each checked by div32Ok; when a check fails, the division takes the two limbs one at a time instead.
