    Comba multiplication #

    The schoolbook product column by column, after Paul Comba: limb k of a * b comes from the sum of the products aᵢ b_{k-i}, plus the carry from the column below. Within a column the products are independent, so there is no carry chain from one product to the next as in the rows of addMul1. For short operands the low and high halves of the products go into two UInt64 accumulators, which cannot overflow for fewer than 2³¹ products, and the C compiler vectorizes the loop (colLoop); from 32 limbs the whole products go into one accumulator that counts its wraps (colLoopW), an addition with carry per product, which is faster although not vectorized.

    The specification is Σ_k βᵏ colSum k = a * b (weighted_eq), where colSum k adds the products of column k over all i ≤ k, with limbs past the end read as zero.

                def MPN.W32.combaMul (w : Nat) (a b : ScalarArray.UInt32Array.Slice) (h : CombaOK a b) :
                ScalarArray.UInt32Array

                All a.size + b.size limbs of a * b by Comba's method, one column at a time, into a.size + b.size + 1 limbs whose top limb, which only held a carry, is dropped. The columns come from combaOuter when either operand is shorter than w limbs and from combaOuterW otherwise: the column loop of combaOuterW adds whole products and counts the wraps of its sum, which is 5–13% faster from 32 limbs on Zen 3, but setting up its unrolled loop for each column made products of 2 to 16 limbs 5–8% slower, also when both loops shared one function and chose by column length.
