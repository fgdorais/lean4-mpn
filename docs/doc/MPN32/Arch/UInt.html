    Double-width word operations #

    The primitive operations of limb arithmetic on fixed-width unsigned integers:

    • addWithCarry x y c is x + y + c, as its low word and a carry out;
    • subWithBorrow x y b is x - y - b, as its low word (wrapping) and a borrow out;
    • mulAddAdd x y a b is x * y + a + b, as its low word and its high word.

    The last one cannot overflow since (2ⁿ - 1) * (2ⁿ - 1) + (2ⁿ - 1) + (2ⁿ - 1) = 2²ⁿ - 1. Each type computes in the next wider type, and each operation comes with its specification in Nat.

                    UInt32 × UInt32

                    x * y + a + b as its low word and its high word. In a loop, b is the carry, and it joins last, after p = x y + a, which is also used for the low word: with x y + a + b written as one sum, LLVM adds the carry first, which puts the multiplication on the carry's dependency chain (on AArch64 the multiply-accumulate umaddl, a chain of about 5 cycles per limb, against 2 here).

                                High words of UInt32 #

                                The high-word operations of main64's limb loops, for its flat-array FFT on 32-bit limbs: addh c x y adds the carry out of x + y to c, subh b x y adds the borrow out of x - y to b, mulh x y is the high word of x * y, and div2 hi lo d is the quotient of the double word hi lo by d. They compute in UInt64.
