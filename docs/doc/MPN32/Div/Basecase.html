    Schoolbook division #

    divRemBasecase t u v divides u by a divisor v of at least two limbs with a nonzero top limb, by Knuth's Algorithm D, like GMP's schoolbook division, through main's MPN.Div.Basecase on UInt32Array (Schoolbook.divRemN): u and v normalized by a shift, then two quotient limbs per step from the top down, by one pass of two rows, from config.div2Rows limbs in the divisor, in one array, and the remainder shifted back. Its multiply-subtract pass carries the biased borrow of subMulBiased.

    The estimate of a quotient limb from the top three limbs of the window and the top two of the divisor (qEstimate3) is the one of Div.Two's division by two limbs.

        @[inline]
        def MPN.W32.qEstimate3 (q wt wn wl vt vs : UInt32) :
        UInt32

        The estimate q of a quotient limb, from qEstimate, corrected twice with qRefine using the next limb wl of the window and the second limb vs of the divisor. Then it is at most one too large, and rarely, so that v is seldom added back.

        It takes q as a parameter and computes wt β with a shift: when the kernel compares terms that contain a multiplication by the literal 2³², it may unfold it into a recursion on the literal.

            def MPN.W32.divRemBasecase (t : USize) (u v : ScalarArray.UInt32Array.Slice) (hn : 2 ≤ Std.Slice.size v) :
            ScalarArray.UInt32Array × ScalarArray.UInt32Array

            u / v and u % v by Knuth's Algorithm D, for a divisor of at least two limbs, untrimmed, by main's Schoolbook.divRemN: u and v normalized by a shift, the quotient limbs from the top down in one array, from t limbs in the divisor by GMP's 3-by-2 steps with the reciprocal WordArray.recip32, checked, and the remainder shifted back. It is exact when the top limb of v is not zero. A dividend of fewer limbs than v is its own remainder; were the slices too large for USize indices, which none is, the results would come from the values instead.
