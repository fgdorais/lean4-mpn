# Lean 4 / MPN

Verified multi-precision arithmetic for Lean 4, in the style of GMP's `mpn` functions. Every
operation is proved correct against `Nat`.

## Layout

One package, three libraries:

| library | directory | namespace | contents |
|---|---|---|---|
| `MPN` | `MPN/` | `MPN` | the part that does not depend on the width of a limb: the classes of limb arrays (`LimbArray`, `WordArray`, …), their generic kernels, the Karatsuba, Toom and FFT products, the generic division, square root, gcd and radix conversion (`NatOps`), and `GNat` |
| `MPN32` | `MPN32/` | `MPN.W32` | a GMP-free `Nat` on `UInt32` limbs, `MPN.W32.Nat`, in pure Lean with the full set of algorithms; see [MPN32/README.md](MPN32/README.md) |
| `MPN64` | `MPN64/` | `MPN.W64` | `UInt64` limbs, `MPN.W64.Nat`, with the high words of sums and products in inline C (`__uint128_t`), within a few times GMP's speed; see [MPN64/README.md](MPN64/README.md) |

**Portability rule.** `MPN32` and the shared `MPN` build wherever Lean does, with nothing but Lean
and its runtime: no `@[extern]`, `extern c inline` or `implemented_by` of their own, no import of
`MPN64`, and in their one dependency, lean4-scalar-arrays, only the runtime's functions of `lean.h`
in standard C. This is the only reason for 32-bit limbs, so a change that breaks it does not
belong in them; anything faster that needs C goes in `MPN64`. CI checks it twice: by inspection
(`scripts/check_portable.py`), and by building `MPN32` and running its tests on Linux arm64, macOS
arm64 and Windows.

`MPN.Default` (the library `MPNDefault`) makes `MPN.Nat` the natural numbers on 64-bit limbs. Both
widths build in one environment, and the tests (`lake test`) check both. The branches `main32` and
`main64`, which held the two widths before, are frozen.

## Using it

```toml
[[require]]
name = "lean4-mpn"
git = "https://github.com/fgdorais/lean4-mpn"
rev = "main"
```

then `import MPN.Default` for `MPN.Nat`, or `import MPN64` / `import MPN32` for a width. The
thresholds of each width are options of its library, with the prefix `w32_` or `w64_`, for
example `options = { march = "native", w64_mulKaratsuba = "48" }`; `lakefile.lean` lists them.

```
lake build                     # the libraries and the tests
lake test
lake build bench64 tune64      # the executables, which `lake build` does not build
.lake/build/bin/bench64        # see MPN64/BENCH.md; bench32 and MPN32/BENCH.md for 32-bit limbs
.lake/build/bin/tune64         # the thresholds for this machine
```

## The shared modules

| module | contents |
|---|---|
| `MPN.Sum` | finite sums `sumRange f n`, for column sums of products and the inverse FFT |
| `MPN.NatMod` | congruences modulo the Fermat numbers of the FFT |
| `MPN.Poly` | coefficient lists and their values `polyVal x cs`: sums, products, even and odd parts |
| `MPN.LimbArray` | `LimbArray A L`: arrays of limbs of `w` bits, and `valAt`, the value of a range of limbs, with its lemmas, for limbs of any width |
| `MPN.FFT.Roots` | the inversion of the FFT's transform modulo a Fermat number, on natural numbers |
| `MPN.FFT.Algebra` | the algebra of the transforms modulo `2ᴺ + 1` for any `N`: bit reversal, the butterflies, and the coefficients they give (`fft_coef`, and `fft_coef_cyc` for products modulo `x^K - 1`) |
| `MPN.FFT.Windows` | the forward and inverse transforms with their proofs, in place on windows of `n + 1` limbs of one array, over the class `WinOps` of residue operations modulo `2^(w n) + 1` on a `LimbArray`, for limbs of any width `w` |
| `MPN.FFT.Product` | Schönhage–Strassen products, products modulo `2^(w N) - 1` and squares on one flat array, with their proofs, over `WinOps` and the class `FFTOps` of the other in-place operations, the pointwise products an argument |
| `MPN.Toom.Ops` | `ToomOps A L`: the in-place passes of the Karatsuba and Toom methods on a `LimbArray`, the products below their thresholds and Karatsuba's middle term, with their specifications; `Toom.Config`, the thresholds |
| `MPN.Toom.Karatsuba` | Karatsuba's products and squares over `ToomOps`, with their proofs, for limbs of any width |
| `MPN.Toom.Steps` | the passes of `ToomOps` as equations on values, and the recombination of a product from its coefficients |
| `MPN.Toom.Interp` | the interpolations of degree 3, 4, 5, 6, 10 and 14 at natural points, in place, with their proofs, generated by `scripts/toom_interp.py` |
| `MPN.Toom.Toom` | the pieces of an operand, their evaluations at a point, and the products at the points |
| `MPN.Toom.Methods` | Toom-3, Toom-3 by 2, Toom-4 by 2, Toom-4, Toom-4 by 3, Toom-6, Toom-8 and the squares of Toom-3, Toom-4, Toom-6 and Toom-8, with their proofs, generated by `scripts/toom_methods.py` |
| `MPN.Toom.Mul` | `mulToom` and `sqrToom`: the choice of method by size and shape, recursively, down to Karatsuba |
| `MPN.WordGcd` | Euclid's algorithm on words, and the runs of Lehmer's steps on leading words |
| `MPN.Generic.Ops` | `NatOps α`: the operations of a representation of numbers at a base, with their specifications |
| `MPN.Generic.Barrett` | division by a reciprocal from Newton's iteration over `NatOps`, every estimate checked |
| `MPN.Generic.DC` | divide-and-conquer division (Burnikel and Ziegler) over `NatOps`, with the instance's base case |
| `MPN.Generic.Sqrt` | Zimmermann's Karatsuba square root over `NatOps`, with the instance's base case, and facts about `Nat.sqrt` |
| `MPN.Generic.HGCD` | half-gcd over `NatOps` (Schönhage, Möller), with the instance's base case |
| `MPN.Generic.Radix` | decimal strings by divide and conquer over `NatOps` |
| `MPN.Trim` | trimming (`trimTo k`, `isTrimmedTo k`), the limbs of a `Nat` by halves (`ofNatW`, `ofNatLen`), and comparison of windows and arrays (`ltWin`, `ltA`) on any `WordArray`, with `valAt` as their specification |
| `MPN.Dispatch` | the products and squares of both widths, written into a window: the choice between the FFT, the Toom methods and unbalanced pieces by the thresholds of a `MulConfig`; the wrapped product modulo `β^N - 1` of Barrett's division (`mulModW`) |
| `MPN.Bridge` | `Bridge A α`, a width's `NatOps α` with the conversions from and to `GNat A`, and over it Zimmermann's square root (`sqrtZ`), half-gcd and Lehmer's step on `GNat` |
| `MPN.Dispatch.Div` | the choice of a division of normalized operands over `NatOps` (divide and conquer, Barrett's division, the quotient alone) by the thresholds of a `DivConfig`, and of a square root over `GNat` (`SqrtConfig`) |

An algorithm written and proved over `NatOps` serves both libraries with only a new instance.
What each width keeps of its own is in its `Arch/` directory, its hand-tuned kernels (Comba's
products, the rows of the schoolbook product and square, division by two limbs, the high words in
C on 64-bit limbs), and in the glue between those kernels and the shared code.

## Benches and tuners

`Bench.Harness` and `Tune.Harness` (the library `Harness`) are the parts of the benches and tuners
that both widths share: the timing, the rows, the command line, the rows of `MPN.W32.Nat` and
`MPN.W64.Nat` against `Nat` (`bigRow`), and the crossover search. `Tune.Shared` tunes the
thresholds of the shared dispatchers once for any width (`tuneMul`, `tuneSqr`, `tuneDiv`). The
tuners measure size crossovers; the choices of algorithm follow GMP's on modern platforms.
